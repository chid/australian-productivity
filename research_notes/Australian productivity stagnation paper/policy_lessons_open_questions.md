## 1. Australia: the PC five-pillar inquiries (2025), the Economic Reform Roundtable, and the government response

### Takeaway
The "47 vs 71" conflict is settled. **47** is the total number of recommendations across the five 2025 pillar final reports, released on **19 December 2025**. **71** is the count from the PC's 2023 *5-year Productivity Inquiry: Advancing Prosperity*, which had 29 reform directives and 71 recommendations in nine volumes. Sources that give 71 for 2025 are confusing the two inquiries.

By the May 2026 Budget the government said it was "progressing 13 of the 17 reform areas" the PC highlighted. Measured productivity is still flat: labour productivity was −0.2% over the year to June 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Inquiry structure and titles [V]**
- The five pillar inquiries and their final reports, all released 19 December 2025:
  1. *Creating a more dynamic and resilient economy*
  2. *Building a skilled and adaptable workforce*
  3. *Harnessing data and digital technologies* (Inquiry Report no. 111, December 2025)
  4. *Delivering quality care more efficiently*
  5. *Investing in cheaper, cleaner energy and the net zero transformation*

  The PC also published a summary paper, *Meeting the productivity challenge*, which lists all recommendations and an implementation timeline. — [PC, Meeting the productivity challenge](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/meeting-productivity-challenge/); [PC five pillars hub](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/); [Data & digital final report PDF](https://assets.pc.gov.au/2025-12/data-digital_0.pdf)
- People: Chair Danielle Wood; Deputy Chair Alex Robson. The PC hub page does not credit individual authors for each report. — [PC](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/meeting-productivity-challenge/)
- **Total: 47 reform recommendations across the five inquiries.** The PC hub states this figure, and so does the trade press. — [PC hub](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/); [Financial Standard, "Productivity Commission makes 47 recommendations"](https://www.financialstandard.com.au/news/productivity-commission-makes-47-recommendations-179811060)
- **Source of "71":** The 2023 *Advancing Prosperity* inquiry had "29 reform directives, 71 recommendations spanning nine volumes". The government said it was progressing, in some form, more than two-thirds of the 29 directives. **This is contested:** an ABC analysis quoted a source involved in preparing the report who said essentially nothing had been done on the 71. — [Treasurer media release, PC 5-year inquiry (2023)](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/jim-chalmers-2022/media-releases/productivity-commission-5-year-inquiry); [PC Productivity inquiry 2023](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/productivity/); [ABC News, 31 Mar 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-03-31/federal-election-housing-productivity-bandaid-solutions-budget/105098402) [S]
- Process [S]:
  - Before drafting, the PC consulted on 15 reform areas. — [Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/productivity-commission-calls-for-priority-reform-submissions-12cc9ea9-3705-4272-9f41-40e1a14c9de9/)
  - The interim reports came out progressively between 31 July and 13 August 2025, alongside an overview, *Growth mindset: how to boost Australia's productivity*. — [PC Growth mindset](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/growth-mindset)
  - The "17 reform areas" in the 2026 Budget statement is the PC's own framing of the final package. I did not see the full list of 17.

**Headline final recommendations and estimated gains [V unless marked]**
- *Dynamic economy:*
  - A hybrid corporate tax system: a 20% company rate for firms with up to $1bn turnover, 28% for larger firms, plus a 5% net cashflow tax on all companies [S]. Estimated GDP gain: just over **$13bn (0.7%)**.
  - A whole-of-government regulatory agenda targeting a **$10bn** cut in regulatory burden.
  
  — [PC](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/meeting-productivity-challenge/); [Accounting Times](https://www.accountingtimes.com.au/tax/productivity-commission-calls-for-corporate-tax-and-regulatory-overhaul) [S]
- *Data & digital:*
  - A potential **$116bn** productivity opportunity over a decade from AI.
  - AI-specific regulation only as a "last resort".
  - Amend the Privacy Act 1988 to embed an outcomes-based "fair and reasonable" test.
  - Monitor AI's effect on copyright over three years (Rec 2.1).
  
  — [PC](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/meeting-productivity-challenge/); [King & Wood Mallesons](https://www.mallesons.com/au/en/insights/latest-thinking/productivity-commission-releases-final-report-into-harnessing-data-and-digital-technologies.html) [S]; [Copyright Agency](https://www.copyright.com.au/2025/12/productivity-commission-reports-released/) [S]
- *Care:* A National Prevention Investment Framework. Spending $1.5bn over five years is estimated to return about $2.7bn in government savings ten years later. — [PC](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/meeting-productivity-challenge/)
- *Energy:* Better siting of electricity generation and storage is estimated to save about **$8bn over 15 years**. — [PC](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/meeting-productivity-challenge/)
- *Workforce:* National frameworks to assess the quality of teaching resources and teacher professional development. — [Financial Standard](https://www.financialstandard.com.au/news/productivity-commission-makes-47-recommendations-179811060) [S]

**Economic Reform Roundtable, 19–21 August 2025**
- The roundtable was held at Parliament House and was invitation-only. Its three themes were:
  - making the economy more productive
  - building resilience in the face of global uncertainty
  - strengthening budget sustainability

  Treasury published theme papers on productivity, resilience, and budget sustainability and tax reform, plus a regulator engagement page. — [Treasury, Economic Reform Roundtable](https://www.treasury.gov.au/review/economic-reform-roundtable) [V]; [Treasurer opening remarks, 19 Aug 2025](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/jim-chalmers-2022/speeches/opening-remarks-economic-reform-roundtable-parliament-house)

- **Contested:** A leaked Treasury document recommended outcomes in advance, including deregulation and faster housing approvals. The Opposition called the event "choreographed". — [Accounting Times](https://www.accountingtimes.com.au/economy/treasury-leak-sparks-concerns-of-choreographed-roundtable) [S]
- **(advocacy)** CPA Australia welcomed the "quick-win opportunities". — [Medianet, 22 Aug 2025](https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2025/08/cpa-australia-welcomes-quick-win-opportunities-as-productivity-reform-journey-begins/115112) [S]

**2026 developments [V]**
- The PC *Productivity update – September 2026* (Alex Robson, 3 Sep 2026) reports:
  - In the June 2026 quarter, output and hours both rose 0.4%, so labour productivity was flat. The March quarter was −0.6%.
  - Over the year to June 2026, output rose 2.1% and hours about 2.3%, so labour productivity fell 0.2%. The chart gives hours as 2.4%, which is internally inconsistent with the text.
  - Market-sector productivity was +0.2% in the quarter and −0.1% over the year.
  - Non-market productivity was −0.3% over the year and is **below its March 2007 level**.
  - Since June 2014: market sector +7.5%, non-market −2.5%, whole economy +3.6%.
  - Productivity is about 1% above its 2015–19 average.
  - **May 2026 Budget:** the government said it was "progressing 13 of the 17 reform areas" from the five pillars. The PC does not say which four were not taken up.
  - The PC currently has inquiries running on housing supply, business dynamism and reporting requirements.

  — [PC Productivity update Sept 2026](https://www.pc.gov.au/ongoing/productivity-insights/update-september-2026/)
- Background brief: [APH Parliamentary Library, "Australia's flagging productivity growth" (Policy Brief 2025–26)](https://www.aph.gov.au/About_Parliament/Parliamentary_departments/Parliamentary_Library/Research/Policy_Briefs/2025-26/Australiasflaggingproductivitygrowth) [S]

**National Competition Policy revival [V/S]**
- Treasurers signed a 10-year revitalised NCP agreement in **November 2024**. It is backed by a **$900m National Productivity Fund**, which pays states for delivering reforms. — [Treasurer media release, "National agreement to revitalise competition"](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/jim-chalmers-2022/media-releases/national-agreement-revitalise-competition-consumers); [Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/federal-state-and-territory-treasurers-agree-to-reform-competition-policy-ba4e2446-e00f-4bac-b460-f924b3d472c5/)
- PC modelling in *National Competition Policy: modelling proposed reforms* (final report released 29 Nov 2024) estimates the reforms could add **$26–45bn to GDP (1.0–1.7%)** in 2023–24 dollars and lower prices by about 1.45 percentage points. The government quoted the upper bound: "up to $45bn … around $5,000 per household". Critics, and Chalmers himself, acknowledged that the upper bound assumes every opportunity is pursued. — [PC NCP analysis report](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/competition-analysis/report); [The Mandarin](https://www.themandarin.com.au/282782-competition-reforms-could-add-45bn-to-gdp-if-implemented-properly) [S]; [Budget 2025–26, Statement on building a stronger economy](https://budget.gov.au/content/06-economy.htm)

### Inferences
- In the paper, write "47 final recommendations (PC 2025)" and "71 recommendations / 29 directives (PC 2023)". The two figures describe different inquiries; they are not competing counts of the same thing.
- The PC's gain estimates come from different methods: CGE modelling for tax and NCP, cost–benefit analysis for energy and prevention, and scenario estimates for AI. They are not additive and should not be summed.
- There is a recurring gap between recommendation and implementation. The ABC's claim about the 2023 report and the 13/17 figure in 2026 together suggest "progressing" is a weak standard. This could be a research angle: an implementation tracker similar to EPIC's Draghi Observatory.

### Gaps
- I could not see the per-report split of the 47 recommendations, the list of 17 reform areas, or which 4 areas were not progressed. *Meeting the productivity challenge* (PDF) should contain these.
- I found no consolidated official list of roundtable outcomes and did not retrieve the three tax principles. The 21 Aug 2025 press conference transcript on the Treasury page is the primary source to check.
- I did not find a formal written government response to the December 2025 reports, only the Budget statement.

## 2. Earlier reform eras: Hilmer, the 1990s NCP dividend, and lessons for today

### Takeaway
The PC's 2005 review credited NCP and related reforms with raising GDP by about **2.5% (around $20bn a year)** and helping drive the 1990s productivity surge. Since then, researchers have questioned how much of that surge was caused by the reforms. The design feature most relevant today is the payment-for-reform mechanism, which the 2024 National Productivity Fund deliberately copies.

### Cited Findings
- Hilmer, F.G., Rayner, M.R. & Taperell, G.Q. (1993), *National Competition Policy: Report by the Independent Committee of Inquiry*, AGPS, Canberra. This report led to the 1995 Competition Principles Agreement and to competition payments to the states. [K] — [NCP archive (ncp.ncc.gov.au)](http://ncp.ncc.gov.au/docs/National%20Competition%20Policy%20Review%20report,%20The%20Hilmer%20Report,%20August%201993.pdf)
- Productivity Commission (2005), *Review of National Competition Policy Reforms*, Inquiry Report no. 33, Canberra (dated 28 Feb, released April 2005). Findings:
  - NCP and related reforms raised GDP by about 2.5%, or around $20bn.
  - The reforms contributed to the 1990s productivity surge, with large gains in electricity, gas, telecommunications, ports and rail.
  - The benefits were unevenly distributed across regions.
  
  [K] — [PC report page](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/national-competition-policy/report)
- The 2024 revival explicitly restores the payment-for-reform model through the $900m Fund. — [Treasurer media release](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/jim-chalmers-2022/media-releases/national-agreement-revitalise-competition-consumers) [S]
- Harper, I. et al. (2015), *Competition Policy Review: Final Report*, Commonwealth of Australia. This was the precursor to the "NCP 2.0" agenda, which was largely not implemented by the states until 2024. [K] — [Treasury](https://treasury.gov.au/publication/p2015-cpr-final-report)

### Inferences
- Several lessons apply to today's agenda:
  - Hilmer-era gains were concentrated in network industries under government ownership.
  - Today's frontier is services, care, housing/planning and regulatory burden, where measured gains are harder to estimate ex ante and harder to attribute ex post.
  - Conditional fiscal transfers worked because they were independently assessed by the National Competition Council. Whether the 2024 Fund has a comparable independent assessor is worth checking.

### Gaps
- I did not re-verify the 2005 figures (2.5%, $20bn) in this session. Check the PC report's Overview before quoting them.
- I did not retrieve ex-post econometric critiques of the NCP–productivity attribution. Candidates to search: Quiggin (various), Parham (2004) on the 1990s surge.

## 3. Canada: Bank of Canada warnings, internal trade, and the productivity super-deduction

### Takeaway
Canada's policy response has three parts: central-bank agenda-setting (2024), legislation to reduce internal trade barriers (2025), and investment tax incentives in Budget 2025. The super-deduction is aimed at the investment-per-worker gap. No ex-post evaluation exists yet, because the measures are too recent.

### Cited Findings
- Senior Deputy Governor Carolyn Rogers, "Time to break the glass: Fixing Canada's productivity problem", Halifax Partnership, 26 March 2024. She called productivity an "emergency". [K] — [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2024/03/time-to-break-glass-fixing-canada-productivity-problem/)
- Budget 2025 (4 Nov 2025), "productivity super-deduction":
  - Enhanced or immediate capital cost allowance measures.
  - The budget says it lowers Canada's marginal effective tax rate from **15.6% to 13.2%**.
  - Reported cost: $45m in 2025–26, rising to $280m, about **$1.5bn over five years**. It is unclear which measures this figure combines; verify against Budget Annex.
  
  — [Advisor.ca](https://advisor.ca/news/budgets-productivity-super-deduction-looks-to-boost-capital-investment/) [S]; [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/consumer-markets-perspectives/budget-2025-includes-productivity-super-deduction-tax-incentives-and-elimination-of-luxury-tax-on-certain-aircraft-and-vessels) [S]; [PMO, 7 Nov 2025](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2025/11/07/prime-minister-carney-outlines-budget-2025-measures-enable-1-trillion) [S]
- Internal trade: the *Free Trade and Labour Mobility in Canada Act* (Part 1 of Bill C-5, *One Canadian Economy Act*, Royal Assent June 2025) removes federal exceptions under the CFTA and recognises provincial goods, services and labour standards. [K] — [Parliament of Canada, LEGISinfo C-5 (45-1)](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-5)
- Estimated gains: Alvarez, Krznar & Tombe (2019), "Internal Trade in Canada: Case for Liberalization", IMF Working Paper 19/158, estimate that full liberalisation could raise GDP by about 4%. [K] — [IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2019/07/22/Internal-Trade-in-Canada-Case-for-Liberalization-47100)

### Inferences
- Canada's internal-trade reform is the closest analogue to Australia's Mutual Recognition Acts (1992) and Automatic Mutual Recognition of occupational licences (2021). Australia has decades of experience with mutual recognition that Canada could learn from, and an evaluation of AMR uptake in Australia would be a useful contribution.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the official Budget 2025 costing table for the super-deduction, or a list of which provinces have signed mutual-recognition arrangements.
- No independent evaluation exists yet, from the PBO or academics, of either measure's productivity effect.

## 4. EU and UK: Draghi, the Competitiveness Compass, the Savings and Investments Union, the UK Industrial Strategy and planning reform

### Takeaway
The EU's diagnosis is well documented and widely accepted. Delivery is slow: EPIC's tracker shows about 11% of Draghi's 383 recommendations fully implemented after one year and about 15% by January 2026. The UK has a sector-focused Industrial Strategy and planning-system reform. Their effect on productivity is not yet measurable.

### Cited Findings
- Draghi, M. (2024), *The future of European competitiveness*, European Commission, 9 Sep 2024. It estimates additional investment needs of €750–800bn a year (about 4.4–4.7% of EU GDP) and contains 383 recommendations. [K for the investment figures] — [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/eu-competitiveness/draghi-report_en)
- Implementation trackers. Their methods differ, so the rates are not comparable:
  - **EPIC Draghi Observatory (Sept 2025):** 43 of 383 recommendations (11.2%) fully implemented, 20.1% partially.
  - **EPIC (Jan 2026):** about 15.1% fully, about 38.9% fully or partially.
  - **Institut Montaigne:** about 30% on a broader measure.
  - **Banco de España (July 2026):** progress uneven across pillars, faster in the Savings and Investments Union area.
  
  — [Wikipedia summary, Draghi report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draghi_report) [S]; [Eunews, Sept 2025](https://www.eunews.it/en/2025/09/03/nestoras-epic-one-year-after-the-draghi-report-a-sense-of-urgency-is-still-lacking) [S]; [Institut Montaigne](https://institutmontaigne.org/en/publications/implementing-draghi-report-moment-truth) [S]; [Banco de España blog, "The Draghi Report, two years on"](https://www.bde.es/wbe/en/noticias-eventos/blog/el-informe-draghi-dos-anos-despues-que-se-ha-cumplido.html) [S]
- Draghi said in September 2025 that the EU had fallen further behind because of "complacency". — [Irish Times, 16 Sep 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/09/16/eu-economy-falls-behind-global-rivals-due-to-complacency-warns-mario-draghi/) [S]
- **(advocacy)** Encompass Europe (July 2026) calls for a European Competitiveness Act. — [Encompass](https://encompass-europe.com/comment/draghi-has-won-the-argument-now-europe-needs-a-competitiveness-act) [S]
- European Commission (2025), *A Competitiveness Compass for the EU*, COM(2025) 30, 29 Jan 2025. It has three pillars: closing the innovation gap, decarbonisation with competitiveness, and reducing dependencies. It is accompanied by "omnibus" simplification packages. [K] — [EC](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/eu-competitiveness/competitiveness-compass_en)
- European Commission (2025), *Savings and Investments Union*, COM(2025) 124, 19 Mar 2025. [K] — [EC](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/regulation-and-supervision/savings-and-investments-union_en)
- UK Department for Business and Trade (2025), *The UK's Modern Industrial Strategy*, 23 June 2025, CP 1338. It targets eight "IS-8" growth-driving sectors. [K] — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uks-modern-industrial-strategy-2025)
- UK planning: the Planning and Infrastructure Bill (2025) and the revised NPPF (Dec 2024), with a target of 1.5m homes. The OBR estimated the Dec 2024 planning reforms could raise real GDP by about 0.2% by 2029–30 and about 0.4% within ten years. [K] — [OBR, Planning reforms paper, March 2025](https://obr.uk/)

### Inferences
- The EU case shows that a high-profile diagnostic report does not translate into reform without an enforcement mechanism. This is directly relevant to the PC's 47 recommendations. The paper could propose an "Australian Draghi Observatory" that scores implementation of PC recommendations.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the date of Royal Assent for the UK Planning and Infrastructure Act, or the OBR figures, in this session.
- I found no causal evaluation of the UK Industrial Strategy's productivity effects; it is too early.

## 5. United States: CHIPS & Science, the IRA, deregulation, and contested evidence on industrial policy

### Takeaway
US industrial policy produced a large rise in announced manufacturing construction investment. It has not yet shown aggregate productivity gains. Academic evidence on industrial policy has moved from blanket scepticism to careful, context-dependent support, mostly from historical cases. Post-2025 policy has partly shifted towards tariffs and deregulation and away from IRA subsidies.

### Cited Findings [K: not re-fetched this session]
- CHIPS and Science Act, Pub. L. 117-167 (9 Aug 2022): about $52.7bn for semiconductor manufacturing and R&D, plus a 25% advanced manufacturing investment tax credit. Inflation Reduction Act, Pub. L. 117-169 (16 Aug 2022). — [Congress.gov H.R.4346](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/4346); [Congress.gov H.R.5376](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/5376)
- Juhász, R., Lane, N. & Rodrik, D. (2024), "The New Economics of Industrial Policy", *Annual Review of Economics* 16: 213–242. doi:10.1146/annurev-economics-081023-024638.
- Lane, N. (2025), "Manufacturing Revolutions: Industrial Policy and Industrialization in South Korea", *Quarterly Journal of Economics* (forthcoming/2025). It finds positive long-run effects of Korea's 1970s heavy and chemical industry drive.
- Juhász, R. (2018), "Temporary Protection and Technology Adoption: Evidence from the Napoleonic Blockade", *American Economic Review* 108(11): 3339–76.
- Sceptical or contested evidence: Irwin, D. (2025 and earlier), PIIE commentary on CHIPS costs per job **(think tank)**. The CBO and the Penn Wharton Budget Model revised the IRA's clean energy credit costs well above the original scoring.
- The OBBBA (July 2025) curtailed several IRA clean-energy credits. [K, verify]

### Inferences
- For Australia's Future Made in Australia (2024) agenda, the US evidence counsels caution: the gains documented in the literature come from export-disciplined, time-limited support, which differs from open-ended production credits.

### Gaps
- I did not search or verify US items in this session because of the tool budget. Every US entry above should be checked before citation, especially the OBBBA details and any 2026 CHIPS evaluations (e.g. GAO, Commerce reports).

## 6. Singapore and Southeast Asia: SkillsFuture, the PSG, the ESR, and Australia's SEA Economic Strategy to 2040

### Takeaway
Singapore's 2026 Economic Strategy Review produced **32 recommendations across eight thrusts**, with a growth aim of the higher end of 2–3% a year over the next decade and an emphasis on AI adaptation and reskilling. Evidence on SkillsFuture and the PSG is mostly administrative. Australia's *Invested* SEA strategy (Moore 2023) is being implemented through trade missions and taskforces. No productivity evaluation of it exists.

### Cited Findings
- **ESR:**
  - Recommendations were submitted 13 May 2026; the final report was released 24 June 2026 (sources differ on the date).
  - 32 measures in eight thrusts: four for growth and four for good jobs.
  - The report proposes reviewing SkillsFuture Jobseeker Support, which does not cover many PMEs (professionals, managers and executives).
  
  — [gov.sg, ESR final recommendations](https://www.gov.sg/features/esr/final-report/) [S]; [The Online Citizen, 13 May 2026](https://theonlinecitizen.com/2026/05/13/singapore-unveils-32-economic-strategy-review-recommendations-for-growth-and-jobs) [S]; [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/economic-strategy-review-committees-release-final-report-to-secure-growth-create-jobs/) [S]; [JTC](https://www.jtc.gov.sg/about-jtc/news-and-stories/feature-stories/singapore-rolls-out-7-strategies-to-secure-growth) [S]
- SkillsFuture (launched 2015) and the Productivity Solutions Grant (launched 2018, administered by Enterprise Singapore). I did not retrieve any peer-reviewed evaluation. [K] — [SkillsFuture](https://www.skillsfuture.gov.sg/); [GoBusiness PSG](https://www.gobusiness.gov.sg/productivity-solutions-grant/)

  - **75 recommendations**.

  - DFAT has published an *Advancing implementation* update.

  — [DFAT strategy page](https://dfat.gov.au/southeastasiaeconomicstrategy); [DFAT, Advancing implementation (PDF)](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/advancing-implementation-invested-southeast-asia-economic-strategy-2040.pdf) [S]; [PM media release](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/southeast-asia-economic-strategy-2040); [PSNews](https://psnews.com.au/?p=118982) [S]

## 7. Open research questions and "further ideas" for the paper

### Takeaway
The most tractable gaps for an Australian economics paper are:
1. Non-market productivity measurement, now that the non-market sector is below its 2007 level.
2. Firm-level diffusion using BLADE.
3. Diffusion lags for AI and intangibles (the J-curve).
4. Regional, state and agglomeration effects tied to housing and planning.
5. The decoupling of productivity and real wages.
6. A systematic tracker of whether PC recommendations are implemented.

### Cited Findings
- **Non-market measurement:**
  - Since June 2014, non-market productivity is down 2.5% and below its 2007 level. — [PC Sept 2026 update](https://www.pc.gov.au/ongoing/productivity-insights/update-september-2026/) [V]
  - The ABS measures much of the non-market sector by inputs, so measured productivity growth there is close to zero by construction. The PC 2023 Volume 5 discusses this. [K] — [PC Advancing Prosperity](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/productivity/)
- **AI and intangibles diffusion lags:** Brynjolfsson, E., Rock, D. & Syverson, C. (2021), "The Productivity J-Curve: How Intangibles Complement General Purpose Technologies", *AEJ: Macroeconomics* 13(1): 333–372, doi:10.1257/mac.20180386. [K] The PC's estimate of a $116bn AI opportunity is a scenario figure, so testing it against J-curve dynamics is a natural extension. — [PC](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/five-productivity-inquiries/meeting-productivity-challenge/)

### Inferences (suggested research designs)
1. **Quality-adjusted non-market output.** Apply outcome-based deflators (e.g. health-adjusted life years, value-added in education) to ABS health and education output. Benchmark against the UK ONS approach following the Atkinson Review.
2. **Quality-adjusted energy productivity.** Adjust energy-sector MFP for reliability and emissions intensity. The net-zero transition may be lowering measured MFP while improving output that current measures do not capture.
3. **Migration–capital interaction.** Ask whether rapid hours growth (hours +2.3% vs output +2.1% in FY26) diluted capital deepening. Use BLADE-linked migrant employment shares and capital per worker.
4. **AI J-curve test.** Use BLADE and the ABS Business Characteristics Survey to estimate lags between adopting AI or intangibles and firm TFP.
5. **Regional and state divergence.** Use state accounts to test whether NCP-era and current reforms produce state-level productivity convergence, and whether the National Productivity Fund's payments correlate with outcomes.
6. **Productivity–wage link.** Decompose the gap between real wages and labour productivity into terms of trade, labour share and price-deflator wedges for 2016–2026.
7. **Recommendation-tracker dataset.** Code every PC recommendation from 2005 to 2025 (including the 71 from 2023 and the 47 from 2025) by implementation status, and relate implementation to sectoral productivity.
