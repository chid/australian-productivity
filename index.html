Australian productivity, compared

Australia's output per hour has barely grown since 2019. The United States has pulled away, Europe has stalled alongside Australia, and much of Southeast Asia is catching up from a far lower base.

Data: Productivity Commission (Sept 2026 update, June 2026 bulletin), OECD Productivity Database, ILOSTAT modelled estimates. Built . Download the data.

1. Australia: the quarterly picture

Labour productivity is output (GDP) divided by hours worked. The COVID-era spike was mostly a composition effect: lower-productivity hours dropped out during lockdowns and came back afterwards. Since 2023 the index has moved sideways.

Caveats

  • Don't mix the OECD and ILO figures. The ILO model puts Australia's 2019–25 growth at about 0.8% a year and its level at 88% of the US. The OECD says 0.1% and 81%. The OECD figures agree with the PC's own data.
  • Level comparisons depend on PPP conversions. Growth rates are more robust than the level rankings. Canada, for example, is 70% of the US on the OECD measure and about 85% on the ILO's.
  • The ILO series runs to 2027, but 2026–27 are projections. They are kept in the CSV and flagged status=projection, but no chart uses them.
  • The PC data has been revised. The September 2026 release revised past index values up by 0.1–0.3 points compared with the June 2026 release. This page uses the latest release.
  • Non-market sector productivity is hard to measure. Much of the output of health, education and public administration is valued by its inputs, which holds measured productivity growth there close to zero.
  • Some labels in the PC chart-data workbooks are stale. They say "September 2025" and name the wrong quarters. This page uses the values, re-labelled from their position in the series.
