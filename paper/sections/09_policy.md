# Policy implications {#sec:policy}

**1. Improve measurement first.** Australia's statistical agencies should routinely publish productivity for the market sector excluding mining, quality-adjusted output for non-market sectors (following the UK's post-Atkinson approach), and mining MFP that accounts for natural resources. This would sharpen accountability without excusing weakness in the market sector [@PC2024health; @ABS2014mining].

**2. Lower the tax cost of investment.** International practice is moving toward cash-flow treatment of investment, in which it is deducted immediately. The United States made 100% bonus depreciation permanent in 2025. In September 2026 Canada proposed a Productivity Mega Deduction, which the government claims would lower its marginal effective tax rate on new investment from 13.0% to 6.4% [@FinCan2026mega]. The PC proposed a company tax rate of 20% for firms under A\$1b plus a 5% net cash-flow tax. This has not been legislated [@PC2025dynamic]. Without reform, Australia's position relative to other countries worsens by default.

**3. Revive competition policy and paid reform.** The PC's 2005 review credited the 1990s National Competition Policy with raising GDP by about 2.5% [@PC2005ncp]. A key design feature was that states were paid for reforms once these were independently assessed. The 2024 revival restores this model through a \$900m National Productivity Fund, with modelled gains of 1.0–1.7% of GDP [@Treasurer2024ncp; @PC2024ncp]. Today the gains lie in services, care, planning and occupational licensing.

**4. Shorten the energy-transition investment phase.** The PC estimates that accelerating approvals for wind generation and transmission by one year would cut electricity bills by about 7% over a decade [@PC2026qbjun].

**5. Reform housing and planning.** Tilting the dwelling mix toward medium density would raise measured construction productivity [@PC2025housing].

**6. Manage the composition of migration.** Visa composition and the use of migrants' skills matter more than headline migration numbers [@e61misallocated2023].

**7. Focus AI policy on diffusion.** Policy should support diffusion to laggard firms and investment in complementary intangible capital, rather than the technology frontier [@PC2025digital].

**8. Implement recommendations already made.** The PC's 2023 five-year inquiry made 71 recommendations, and reporting suggests few have been acted on [@PC2023advancing; @ABC2025implementation]. The five December 2025 reports made 47 more. The government says it is "progressing 13 of the 17 reform areas" [@PC2026update]. The EU's Draghi report illustrates the risk: only about 15% of its 383 recommendations had been fully implemented by January 2026 [@Eunews2025]. The literature on new industrial policy favours support that is time-limited and disciplined by export performance [@JuhaszLaneRodrik2024]. That is relevant to Australia's Future Made in Australia agenda.
