# A research agenda {#sec:agenda}

Seven questions are open and can be answered with Australian data.

1. **A formal bridge between OECD and ABS measures.** No published source decomposes the gap between OECD whole-economy MFP and ABS market-sector MFP into coverage, output concept, timing, labour-input basis and data vintage. Section [2](#sec:data) sketches such a bridge; a full version is the natural next step.
2. **Quality-adjusted non-market output.** Applying outcome-based measures of output (QALYs in health, value added in education) to ABS non-market output, benchmarked against the ONS, would test whether the drag of about 0.3 points a year is economic or statistical.
3. **Migration and capital per hour.** Linking migrant employment shares to firm capital stocks in BLADE would separate capital dilution from misallocation by visa type.
4. **An AI J-curve in firm data.** Event studies of firm TFP after AI and intangible adoption, using the Business Characteristics Survey linked to BLADE, would test the lags assumed in the PC's A\$116b scenario.
5. **Decomposing mining and electricity MFP.** Updating Topp et al. (2008) and applying natural-capital-adjusted MFP [@Topp2008mining] across Australia, Canada and Norway would separate depletion, weather, investment lags and unmeasured quality.
6. **Reallocation regimes after COVID.** Comparing OECD firm-dynamics (DynEmp) entry series and job-to-job flows would test whether Australia's job-retention approach, compared with the US quits surge, explains part of the divergence.
7. **A tracker of PC recommendations.** Coding every PC recommendation since 2005 by whether and when it was implemented, modelled on the EPIC tracker of Draghi's recommendations, would turn the political economy of reform into data. It would also allow ex-ante modelling (such as the 2024 National Competition Policy estimate) to be compared with ex-post outcomes.

Further gaps include the effect of ICP 2017 versus ICP 2021 PPPs on the Australia–US level gap, the difference between Labour Force Survey and Labour Account hours over 2019–25, harmonised AI-adoption rates across countries, and an up-to-date Australian marginal effective tax rate on investment.
